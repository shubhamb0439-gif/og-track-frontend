Series Introduction

The X-Files is an American science fiction horror drama created by Chris Carter that premiered on Fox on September 10, 1993. The series follows FBI Special Agents assigned to the "X-Files" — a designation given to bureau cases involving unexplained, paranormal, or otherwise unclassifiable phenomena, from alien abductions and cryptid encounters to government conspiracies.

Blending "monster-of-the-week" standalone episodes with an ongoing serialized mythology arc about a covert government conspiracy tied to extraterrestrial colonization, the show became a cultural phenomenon of the 1990s, popularizing the tagline "The Truth Is Out There" and influencing a generation of genre television.

The series ran for nine original seasons (1993–2002), was revived for two additional seasons (2016 and 2018), and spawned two theatrical feature films, a short-lived spin-off, and an extended multimedia legacy in comics and games.

Main Characters

Fox Mulder
FBI Special Agent · The Believer

Played by David Duchovny. A brilliant profiler obsessed with proving the existence of extraterrestrial life, driven by the unsolved abduction of his younger sister, Samantha. Known for his unwavering conviction that "the truth is out there."

Dana Scully
FBI Special Agent · The Skeptic

Played by Gillian Anderson. A medical doctor and scientist assigned to debunk Mulder's work, who instead becomes his most trusted partner. Her rational, evidence-based skepticism balances Mulder's intuition throughout the series.

Walter Skinner
Assistant Director, FBI

Played by Mitch Pileggi. Mulder and Scully's direct supervisor, often caught between bureau politics and quietly protecting the agents' investigations.

The Cigarette-Smoking Man
Shadow Conspirator

Played by William B. Davis. A shadowy, chain-smoking government operative and the series' recurring antagonist, tied to nearly every major conspiracy in the mythology arc.

John Doggett
FBI Special Agent (Seasons 8–9)

Played by Robert Patrick. A no-nonsense former New York City police officer who takes over the X-Files after Mulder's abduction, initially doubtful of the paranormal.

Monica Reyes
FBI Special Agent (Seasons 8–9)

Played by Annabeth Gish. An agent with a background in occult crime and a more spiritually open perspective, partnered with Doggett in the show's later seasons.

The Lone Gunmen
Conspiracy Theorist Allies

Melvin Frohike, Richard Langly, and John Fitzgerald Byers — a trio of tech-savvy, fringe-publication conspiracy theorists who frequently assist Mulder and Scully.

Samantha Mulder
Mulder's Missing Sister

Abducted in 1973 when Mulder was twelve years old, her disappearance is the emotional core driving Mulder's lifelong pursuit of the truth.

Plot & Mythology

The X-Files operates on two parallel storytelling tracks:

Monster-of-the-Week episodes — self-contained investigations into cryptids, mutants, ghosts, and unexplained phenomena, often blending horror, dark comedy, and folklore.

The Mythology Arc — a serialized conspiracy narrative involving a secretive cabal called the Syndicate, colluding with extraterrestrial forces on a plan for alien colonization of Earth, using a genetic hybrid substance known as the "Black Oil." The arc explores government cover-ups, human experimentation, alien-human hybrids, and a looming "date of colonization," while Mulder and Scully's search for the truth repeatedly puts them at odds with powerful forces inside their own government.

"I want to believe." — Fox Mulder

Over the course of the series, the central mystery of Samantha Mulder's abduction, the shifting loyalties of shadow-government figures, and Scully's own abduction and mysterious pregnancy deepen the mythology into one of television's most ambitious ongoing conspiracy narratives.

Timeline of Shows & Films

1993 – 1998 · SEASONS 1-5
The X-Files (Original Series Begins)

Premieres on Fox; introduces Mulder, Scully, and the core mythology. Becomes a breakout critical and ratings hit through its first five seasons.

1996 – 1999
Millennium (Related Series by Chris Carter)

A darker companion series from the same creator, following ex-FBI profiler Frank Black. A one-hour crossover episode ties it directly into X-Files lore.

1998
The X-Files: Fight the Future (Feature Film)

Released between Seasons 5 and 6, this theatrical film expands the mythology arc and bridges directly into the ongoing series' story.

1998 – 2002 · SEASONS 6-9
The X-Files (Later Seasons)

The show relocates production to Los Angeles; introduces Agents Doggett and Reyes in Seasons 8–9 following David Duchovny's reduced role and eventual departure.

2001
The Lone Gunmen (Spin-off Series)

A single-season spin-off centered on the fan-favorite conspiracy-theorist trio, set before their X-Files appearances.

2002
Series Finale: "The Truth"

The original nine-season run concludes with Mulder standing trial and the mythology's central conspiracy laid bare — while key questions remain deliberately unresolved.

2008
The X-Files: I Want to Believe (Feature Film)

A standalone film released six years after the finale, focusing on a personal case for Mulder and Scully rather than the alien mythology.

2016 · SEASON 10
The X-Files (Revival, 6 Episodes)

A six-episode miniseries revival reuniting Duchovny and Anderson, reigniting the mythology arc for a new streaming-era audience.

2018 · SEASON 11
The X-Files (Revival, 10 Episodes)

A second revival season further develops the mythology and Mulder and Scully's son, William, closing out the most recent chapter of the saga.

Reboots & Related Releases

2016–2018 Revival (Seasons 10 & 11)

Fox's official revival brought back the original creator and stars, effectively rebooting the mythology for modern audiences across two shortened seasons.

IDW Comic Continuations

IDW Publishing has released multiple ongoing comic series continuing the X-Files story between and beyond the televised seasons, including "Season 10" and "Season 11" comic lines.

Video Games

Tie-in titles include "The X-Files Game" (1998) and later mobile/interactive entries, letting players investigate cases alongside the show's agents.

Future Reboot in Development

A new television take on the franchise has been publicly discussed, with creator Chris Carter and producer Ryan Coogler both linked to development, signaling the mythology's next generation is still to come.
