Help Center

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions about patient assistance and copay programs.

Who is eligible for a patient assistance program? +
Eligibility varies by program, but most require US residency, no or limited prescription drug coverage, and a household income at or below 300-500% of the Federal Poverty Level. Each program listed above has its own specific criteria.
What is the difference between a PAP and a copay program? +
Patient Assistance Programs (PAPs) typically provide medication completely free to uninsured or underinsured patients. Copay assistance programs instead reduce the out-of-pocket cost for patients who already have insurance but face high copays or deductibles.
Can I use a copay card with Medicare or Medicaid? +
Generally, no. Manufacturer copay cards are restricted to patients with commercial/private insurance due to federal anti-kickback rules. Medicare and Medicaid patients should instead look at nonprofit foundation copay grants, which are allowed.
How long does it take to get approved? +
Most manufacturer PAPs take 2-4 weeks to process a completed application. Nonprofit copay foundations often approve grants within a few business days, though funds can close quickly once they run out for the year.
Is there a cost to use DrugAssist Enhanced? +
No. DrugAssist Enhanced is a free directory to help you find and compare assistance programs. We never charge patients for search access, and we don't submit applications on your behalf.
